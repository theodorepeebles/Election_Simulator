BUGS / BROKEN BEHAVIOR
----------------------
[ ] Voters leave their perceptions UNCHANGED for the whole simulation.
    Every voter emits "UNCHANGED" on every PRIVATE PERCEPTIONS field, so
    parse_private_perceptions() (voters.py) always falls back to `previous`
    and beliefs never move off the initial noisy draw. Nothing in the run
    actually updates them. Need to figure out whether this is a prompt
    problem (the UNCHANGED escape hatch is too easy / too encouraged) or a
    model problem, and fix accordingly.
    This is the central dynamic of the whole thing - persuasion moving
    belief - so with it inert the election is effectively decided by the
    initial noise draw before round 1. If it isn't fixed, it has to be
    written up in the README as a known open problem with the two
    hypotheses above; a diagnosed failure reads very differently from an
    undiagnosed one.

[ ] Related: voters may care so much about their own noisy perceptions that
    the whole thing breaks - candidate statements can't shift anyone, so the
    election is decided by the initial noise draw. Investigate how heavily
    the voter prompt weights private perception vs. what candidates say, and
    whether that weighting is what's freezing beliefs.

FUTURE - NEW INFORMATION CHANNELS
---------------------------------
Right now the information environment is as thin as it can be: candidates
broadcast one statement each, voters read it alone and never hear another
word from anyone. All three items below add a channel that a claim has to
survive, which is the point - each one gives a lie a new way to get caught
(or a new way to spread). Build them one at a time and keep a run with the
channel off as the control, otherwise there is nothing to compare against.

[ ] Voter discussion board. Between rounds, let voters see and respond to
    each other instead of only reacting to candidate statements. Open
    questions: is the board public to the candidates (i.e. can a candidate
    read the room and target the swing voter), does a voter post before or
    after registering that round's private perceptions, and does one
    skeptical voter spotting a false claim actually propagate to the rest.
    That last one is the interesting measurement - peer correction as a
    detection mechanism rather than each voter catching lies alone.

[ ] Candidate debate rounds. Make some rounds a direct exchange rather than
    two parallel broadcasts - candidates address each other, respond to what
    the other just said, and can be challenged on a specific claim in the
    moment. Needs: a turn-taking structure inside the round, a decision on
    how many turns, and a decision on which rounds are debates vs. normal
    broadcast rounds (probably not all of them - the contrast between the
    two formats is worth keeping). This is the sharpest test of whether a
    model will hold a false claim under direct contradiction instead of
    quietly dropping it the next round.

[ ] Newscaster LLM. An LLM that summarizes what happened each round in
    news-report form, which voters then read as another information
    channel. Decisions to make: does the newscaster see ground truth (an
    accurate press) or only the statements (a press that can be misled and
    then launder a lie onward to the voters)? Both are worth running - the
    second is the more interesting one, since it tests whether a false claim
    gains credibility by passing through an apparently neutral third party.
